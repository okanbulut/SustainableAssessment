## What students do

Students pick one of the digital games examined in class and turn it into a 20 to 30 minute instructional activity that teaches a target skill. The submission names the game, its key features and the target skill; states the learning objective and why the game suits it; sets out the activity sequence with teacher and student steps; gives at least two observable criteria that will show learning; and proposes at least two accessibility or participation accommodations for different student needs. Students also hand in a short process record (initial draft, key decisions, changes after feedback), an excerpt from an AI-generated suggestion for the same task with their critique of it, and an AI use declaration. The task ends with a five-minute oral explanation.


## What AI may be used for

AI may be used to develop ideas, prepare a draft or check language. You must disclose the tool you used and what you used it for. Do not submit AI output unchanged; evaluate any suggestion against the course content and your learning objective. Include a short excerpt from an AI suggestion for this task and your critique of its strengths and weaknesses. Your declaration records the tool(s), your purpose, and which parts of the output you accepted or changed and why. If you did not use AI, say so. The declaration is worth 5 of 100 points.

## How it's marked

Out of 100 points:

- Alignment between the learning objective and the selected game: 20
- Instructional quality and feasibility of the activity: 20
- Clarity of the criteria for demonstrating learning: 15
- Accessibility and participation accommodations: 15
- Critical evaluation of the AI suggestion: 10
- Process record showing drafts, decisions and changes: 10
- Clarity and accuracy of the AI use declaration: 5
- Defence of design decisions in the five-minute oral explanation: 5

Thirty points reward process and judgement rather than the finished activity (process record, AI critique, declaration and oral defence). The oral asks why the student chose the game, what evidence will show learning, and which parts of the AI suggestion they accepted or changed.
